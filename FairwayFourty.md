## 1. Elevator Pitch

**Fairway After Forty** is a light-hearted sports manga about a full-time working dad who discovers golf in his 40s while balancing marriage, fatherhood, deadlines, snacks, and the suspiciously expensive phrase “just one more bucket.”

The main character joins a company Family Day Charity Golf Scramble after learning the winning team earns a donation toward a children’s playground. He is not naturally gifted. His early swing behaves like office stress wearing golf shoes. But with help from his wife, his 2-year-old daughter, a mysterious range attendant, and a battered copy of Ben Hogan’s **Five Lessons**, he learns that golf is not about swinging harder. It is about fundamentals, sequence, patience, and showing up.

The manga is a comedy underdog story with real golf instruction woven into family life. The core joke is that golf lessons keep appearing in ordinary dad moments: a crayon teaches grip pressure, toy cleanup teaches hip hinge, a stroller teaches takeaway, a playground slide teaches transition, and a toddler yelling “SOF HANS!” saves the first tee shot.

## 2. Core Promise

This manga should feel like:

- A sports manga where the hero’s superpower is stubborn adult consistency.
- A family comedy where golf becomes a shared household language.
- An instructional comic that teaches the broad ideas of Ben Hogan’s five fundamentals without copying his original text or illustrations.
- A warm, funny story about starting something new later in life.

The story is **not** about becoming young again. It is about becoming repeatable.

## 4. Source Inspiration and Copyright Guardrails

The manga uses the lesson structure from Ben Hogan’s book **Five Lessons: The Modern Fundamentals of Golf** as inspiration. The five broad lesson areas are:

1. **The Grip**
2. **Stance and Posture**
3. **The First Part of the Swing**
4. **The Second Part of the Swing**
5. **Summary and Review**

### Important Rights / Originality Rule
Do **not** reproduce Ben Hogan’s original prose, diagrams, page layouts, or exact instructional drawings. Use only high-level, paraphrased fundamentals and original artwork. Any book shown inside the manga should be a simple fictionalized prop unless proper permission has been cleared.

### Practical Treatment
Use wording such as:

- “Inspired by classic golf fundamentals.”
- “Five lessons become five dad-life trials.”
- “The hands, stance, backswing, downswing, and review become story chapters.”

Avoid long direct quotes or copied instructional text. The story’s golf advice should be written in original voice, using the cast’s humor.

## 6. Visual Style Bible

### Overall Art Style
Use the uploaded character reference art as the anchor. The style is:

- Full-color manga / sports-instruction hybrid.
- Clean ink linework with visible sketch texture.
- Watercolor or colored-pencil shading.
- Expressive faces, motion arcs, and comedic reaction panels.
- Clean white gutters and bold black panel borders.
- Instructional callouts, arrows, checklists, and diagrams mixed into story pages.
- Warm slice-of-life backgrounds: office, kitchen, driving range, playground, family home, tournament fairway.

The style should look hand-drawn, polished, and human, not glossy superhero realism.

### Line and Rendering

- Medium-thick black outlines for characters.
- Fine hatching on clothing, arms, face, and golf equipment.
- Soft shading on skin and fabric folds.
- Motion lines for swings, panic, and comedy.
- Speed-line backgrounds for dramatic realizations.
- Small sparkle accents for tiny victories.

### Color Palette

Primary colors:

- **Royal blue polo:** the protagonist’s iconic color.
- **Gray cargo pants:** consistent lower-body silhouette.
- **Bright orange golf shoes:** visual signature and page-to-page continuity marker.
- **White glove:** usually on lead hand.
- **Dark navy accents:** headers, chapter cards, page numbers, instructional boxes.
- **Warm yellow accents:** lesson cards, impact bursts, comedic sound effects.
- **Soft pink:** daughter’s pajamas and family warmth.

### Sound Effects Style

Use large, hand-lettered SFX with color:

- `THWIP!` for clean strike.
- `TAK!` for solid impact.
- `SKRONK!` for ugly mishit.
- `BLAKKA!` for wild comedy drive.
- `WOBBLE!` for unstable stance.
- `CRRRUNCH!` for death grip.
- `SOF HANS!` as daughter’s recurring phrase.

### Page Numbering

Place page number at bottom right in a clean dark font or white number over navy box. Keep consistent.

## 11. Tone and Writing Rules

### Tone

- Funny, warm, light-hearted.
- No melodrama spiral.
- No cruel humiliation.
- No macho sports cliché.
- Sports effort is real, but the stakes are family-friendly.

### Joke Style

Humor comes from:

- Dad overthinking.
- Golf being absurdly technical.
- Toddler wisdom.
- Mina’s grounded comments.
- Brad’s exaggerated confidence.
- Mr. Maru’s tiny cryptic advice.

### Dialogue Style

Keep dialogue short enough for manga bubbles.

Good:

> “Hands too angry.”  
> “They have been through meetings.”

Bad:

> “My current emotional state and accumulated work frustration have transferred into my grip pressure, causing biomechanical inefficiency.”

### Instruction Style

Use simple original phrases:

- “Hold firm, not tight.”
- “Build the base before the swing.”
- “Start together.”
- “Let the body lead.”
- “The ball is witness.”
- “Review the basics.”

Pages 6–28. Grip, stance/posture, and takeaway are learned through range practice and family moments.

Pages 29–40. Downswing sequence emerges. Family scramble begins. Dad-San survives the first tee, then gets greedy and loses one into a bush.

### Act 4: Review, Final Shot, and Home
Pages 41–45. Dad-San reviews the five lessons, makes a mature course decision, hits the meaningful shot, wins the playground donation challenge, and returns home to family life.

## 23. One-Page Continuity Summary for Artists

Draw Dad-San as the same blue-polo, gray-cargo-pants, orange-shoes, glasses-wearing dad golfer from the reference. He is funny, earnest, and learning golf in his 40s while working full time and raising a toddler with his wife Mina. His daughter Pip calls golf balls “moon eggs” and accidentally teaches key lessons. Mr. Maru, the mysterious range attendant, gives short Zen-like golf advice. Brad is the flashy coworker rival who teases but eventually respects him.

The story is about the five fundamentals becoming five life lessons: soft grip, balanced stance, smooth takeaway, sequenced downswing, and reviewing basics under pressure. The tone is warm sports comedy. Keep it bright, readable, instructional, and human. The hero does not need to look perfect. He needs to keep showing up.
